# Product

## Platform

web (installable PWA; phones first, also tablets and classroom laptops)

## Users

Primary: middle- and high-school students learning chemistry and biology, and the teachers who assign them work. They use Assay at home (scanning products in the kitchen, bathroom and laundry room) and in class (a teacher projecting a profile, or students working through a lesson on a laptop).

Secondary: families and everyday shoppers who want to understand a label without fear or jargon.

## Product Purpose

Assay turns the ingredient label on any food, personal-care or cleaning product into a chemistry lesson. Scan a barcode or a label and Assay rates the product, separates organic and natural ingredients from synthetic ones, and explains each molecule: its structure, how it works at the chemical scale and in the body, the reactions it takes part in, the evidence on its safety, and how it is made.

The problem it addresses: chemistry feels abstract to students, while social media fills the gap with fear ("if you can't pronounce it, don't eat it"). Assay teaches dose, mechanism and evidence quality using the products students already own.

Success: a student can explain, in their own words, why an ingredient is in a product, how it works, and how strong the evidence about it is.

## Positioning

Every profile pairs the chemical-scale mechanism with the biological-scale effect, labels the strength of evidence, and links to primary sources. Lessons, quizzes, a myth-or-fact game, a glossary and a molecule collection are built from the same ingredient data, so scanning and learning reinforce each other.

The app works entirely on its own: the curated library, barcode lookup (Open Food/Beauty/Products Facts), on-device OCR and automatic PubChem + PubMed research need no API keys and no human reviewer. An optional AI key deepens profiles for brand-new ingredients.

## Voice

Calm, precise and curious. Explains rather than alarms; never shames food choices; always distinguishes hazard from risk and states what the evidence can and cannot show.

## Constraints

- Educational information, not medical advice.
- No accounts. Learning progress is stored on the device; scan history is keyed to an anonymous device ID and can be deleted.
- Factual content must trace to sources (PubChem, PubMed, regulators); claims are labeled by evidence level.
- Accessibility: WCAG AA contrast in light and dark themes, keyboard-operable everything, reduced-motion support.
